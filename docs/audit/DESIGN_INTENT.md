# Design wave — civic fieldbook

The redesign is gated behind functional verification. It retains Next.js, the existing modular API, PostgreSQL, domain routes and authorization policies.

Evidence-led priorities:

- The old landing page promises more than the product proves. Lead with an understandable journey and show an explicitly illustrative evidence-to-action diagram, not fabricated impact numbers.
- Beneficiaries need one next action, readable bilingual cues and a stable five-step navigation. Use spacious forms and distinct server-confirmed, pending and error states.
- Professional users need their own workspace and trustworthy data states, not a public catalogue of sample personal records.
- Use an editorial civic identity: warm paper, deep ink/teal, restrained saffron accents, crisp borders, large typography and quiet depth. No decorative animation on routine actions.
- All controls must remain keyboard accessible. At 320px, navigation must fit without document overflow. Wide operational tables scroll inside their own region.
- Retain runtime provenance. A polished presentation must never turn demo data, queued changes, pre-screening or draft exports into a claim of certification or sanction.

Verification: mobile/desktop screenshots, overflow and accessibility assertions, existing functional regressions plus browser/API/PostgreSQL checks. The audit is evidence, not a pixel specification.
